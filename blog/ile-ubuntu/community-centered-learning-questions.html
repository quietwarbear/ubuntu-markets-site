Five Questions for Community-Centered Learning

A useful learning plan connects knowledge to people, responsibility, reflection, and the world beyond the assignment.

September 1, 2026 · 4 minute read

1. Who already carries knowledge here?

Begin by identifying the experience learners, families, elders, mentors, and community members already bring. This does not reduce the educator’s responsibility. It gives new knowledge somewhere meaningful to connect.

2. What will learners contribute?

Move beyond what participants will consume or complete. Decide what they will explain, make, document, teach, improve, or return to the community. Contribution gives learning a social purpose.

3. How will responsibility be shared?

Name decisions learners can make, roles they can hold, and ways they can support one another. Shared responsibility should be real enough that the group experiences the consequences of follow-through—and has a process for recovering when something is missed.

4. What evidence of growth matters?

Use more than completion. Look for stronger questions, improved craft, clearer explanation, greater independence, collaborative skill, and the ability to apply learning in a new setting. Invite learners to help interpret that evidence.

5. When will the group reflect together?

Reflection should not be the final five minutes that disappear when time runs short. Schedule it. Ask what the group learned, whose contribution changed the work, where support was needed, and what should happen differently next time.

Planning practice: answer all five questions in one page before choosing the technology. Then use tools only where they make the relationships, commitments, and learning easier to see.

Support learning as a shared practice.
